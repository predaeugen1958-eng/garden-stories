The Duck Who Thought He Was a Chicken

a story for growing hearts (5–8 years)
🐣

On a quiet farm, nestled between a grassy hill and a small, glittering pond, there lived a hen named Hilda.

Hilda was a good mother — patient, warm, and very proud of her eggs. She sat on them for days and days, clucking softly, waiting for them to hatch.

One morning, she felt a crack. Then another. And out of the last egg — not a chick, but a small, yellow duckling with wide, curious eyes.

Hilda blinked.

"Well," she said. "You are different. But you are still mine."

She named him Doug.

🐥

Doug grew up with the chickens. He scratched the ground the way they did. He slept on a perch — though he often wobbled and fell off. And he tried his very best to cluck.

"Cluck-cluck," he would say.

But it never came out quite right. It came out more like a soft, confused "cluck-quack" — which made the other chicks giggle.

"You sound funny, Doug," they said.

Doug felt his feathers puff up.

"I am a chicken," he said. "I just sound a little different."

And the chickens loved him anyway.

🦆

One spring morning, Doug heard something strange.

It came from the pond — a sound he had never heard before, but somehow, it felt familiar.

"Quack! Quack! Quack!"

Doug tilted his head. His feet began to move on their own, carrying him toward the water.

"Where are you going?" called one of the chickens.

"I do not know," said Doug. "But my feet seem to know."

He reached the edge of the pond and looked at the water. It was blue and still, and he felt something deep inside him stir.

"I think I am supposed to go in," he said.

"Chickens do not swim!" said the chickens.

But Doug was not quite a chicken.

He took a breath. He stepped into the water.

💦

And he sank.

Right to the bottom.

He flapped his wings and kicked his feet, and after a moment, he bobbed back up to the surface — spluttering, surprised, and very, very wet.

"Quack!" he said — and it came out perfect.

He floated. He paddled. He felt the water hold him, and for the first time, he felt right.

The chickens gathered at the edge of the pond, watching with wide eyes.

"He is swimming!" they said.

And then, one by one, they began to cheer.

"Go, Doug! Go!"

Doug paddled to the edge and climbed out, dripping but beaming.

"I did it!" he said.

He looked at the pond, then at the chickens, then back at the pond.

"I think I am not a chicken," he said.

"What are you, then?" asked the smallest chick.

Doug thought for a moment.

"I am a duck," he said. "But I am still part of this family."

Hilda, the hen who had raised him, stepped forward and nudged him with her beak.

"Of course you are," she said. "You always were."

🌿

From that day on, Doug spent his mornings in the pond and his afternoons with the chickens.

He taught them how to float — though they never quite got the hang of it.

And they taught him how to scratch for seeds — though he was not very good at it.

But they did it together.

And that was what mattered.
