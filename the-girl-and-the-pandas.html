The Little Girl Who Visited the Panda Family

a story for growing hearts (5–8 years)
🐼

In a quiet valley where bamboo swayed and mist curled around the hills, there lived a family of pandas.

There was Baba — the big panda, strong and gentle, with fur like fresh snow and dark patches like ink spilled on silk. There was Mama — soft and warm, with a patient smile and eyes that crinkled when she spoke. And there was Xiao — the little panda, round and playful, with a habit of tumbling over his own paws.

They lived in a cosy house made of bamboo and thatch, with three bowls, three chairs, and three beds — each one just the right size.

One morning, they made congee for breakfast. It was warm and thick, with red dates and a touch of honey. But it was too hot to eat.

“Let us go for a walk,” said Baba. “By the time we return, it will be cool enough.”

So they went for a stroll through the bamboo grove, leaving their breakfast on the table.

🎋

Not long after, a little girl came walking through the valley. Her name was Mei, and her hair was as dark as night, falling in soft waves past her shoulders.

She had wandered far from her village, following the sound of a bird she had never heard before. She was tired and hungry.

She came upon the panda family's house and knocked softly. No one answered. She pushed the door open — just a crack — and peered inside.

The house was warm, and the smell of congee made her stomach growl. She stepped inside.

Three bowls sat on the table. She tried the biggest one first — but it was too hot, and she burned her tongue. She tried the middle one — but it was too cold, and she shivered. She tried the smallest one — and it was just right. Warm, sweet, and comforting. She ate it all.

🍜

Feeling fuller but still tired, Mei looked around the room.

Three chairs sat by the fire. She tried the biggest one — but it was too hard, and she could not get comfortable. She tried the middle one — but it was too soft, and she sank too deep. She tried the smallest one — and it was just right. It cradled her like a gentle hand. But she sat in it too long, and with a soft creak, it split beneath her.

Mei felt a little bad. She looked toward the bedroom.

Three beds were arranged in a row. She tried the biggest one — but it was too firm. She tried the middle one — but it was too lumpy. She tried the smallest one — and it was just right. Soft and warm, with a cover woven from bamboo leaves. She curled up, closed her eyes, and fell fast asleep.

🌙

When the panda family returned, they noticed the door was open.

“Someone has been here,” said Baba.

They went to the table.

“Someone has tasted my congee,” said Baba, looking at the spoon marks in his bowl.

“And someone has tasted my congee too,” said Mama.

“And someone has eaten all of mine!” said Xiao, holding up his empty bowl with wide eyes.

They looked at the chairs.

“Someone has sat in my chair,” said Baba.

“And in mine,” said Mama.

“And someone broke my chair!” cried Xiao.

They crept quietly into the bedroom.

“Someone has slept in my bed,” said Baba.

“And in mine,” said Mama.

“And someone is still sleeping in my bed!” whispered Xiao.

Mei stirred. She opened her eyes and saw three pandas staring at her. She sat up quickly.

“I am sorry,” she said. “I was tired and hungry. I should not have come in without asking.”

Baba looked at her with kind eyes.

“You are a curious one,” he said. “But you are also honest. That is a good thing.”

Mama stepped forward.

“It is not wrong to be curious,” she said. “But it is important to ask before you take. This is our home. These are our things.”

Xiao smiled at her.

“Did you like my congee?” he asked.

Mei nodded.

“It was the best I have ever had,” she said.

🌿

Mei helped Mama fix the broken chair. She swept the floor and washed the bowls.

And before she left, she bowed to the pandas.

“Thank you for your kindness,” she said. “I will remember to ask before I enter someone’s home.”

Baba placed a small bamboo leaf in her hand.

“You are always welcome here,” he said. “Just knock first.”

Mei smiled, tucked the leaf into her pocket, and walked home through the bamboo grove.

She never forgot the pandas — or the lesson they taught her.
