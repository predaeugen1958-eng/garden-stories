The Little Mouse Who Listened

a story for growing hearts (5–8 years)
🌿

At the base of an old oak tree, where the roots rose like gentle hills and the moss grew thick and soft, there lived a tiny mouse named Pip.

Pip was so small that most animals did not notice him. They walked past without seeing him, flew over without hearing him.

But Pip noticed everything.

He noticed the way the wind moved through the leaves. He noticed which stones held the warmth of the sun longest. He noticed the footsteps of creatures passing by — heavy, light, quick, slow.

And he remembered.

One afternoon, a young squirrel named Hazel came running through the forest. Hazel was not running with joy — she was running with fear.

Hazel had wandered too far from her nest, and now she could not find her way back. She had tried every branch, every fork in the path, but everything looked unfamiliar.

Hazel stopped near the old oak tree, breathing fast.

Pip looked up from his spot among the roots.

“You are lost,” he said quietly.

Hazel jumped. She had not noticed him.

“I am,” she admitted. “I cannot find my tree. I cannot find my nest.”

“Can you make a sound?” Pip asked.

“What kind of sound?” Hazel said.

“The sound you make when you are worried,” Pip said. “The one you make when you want to be found.”

Hazel thought for a moment. Then she let out a sharp, clear call:

“Kuk!”

The sound echoed through the forest.

Pip closed his eyes and listened.

He heard the call travel up, bounce off the bark of nearby trees, and settle. He noticed that it came back softer from the direction of the old oak — which meant Hazel’s nest was not far away.

“Kuk again,” Pip said.

Hazel did.

This time, Pip heard the echo more clearly. It was coming from higher up, and from the left.

“Your nest is in that direction,” Pip said. “High up. Near the fork where the branches spread wide.”

Hazel looked up. She could not see it from here — but she trusted Pip.

“How do you know?” Hazel asked.

“I listened,” Pip said.

Hazel followed the direction Pip gave her. She climbed, slowly at first, then with more ease. And soon, she reached the fork of the old oak — where her nest was waiting, warm and safe.

Hazel looked down and saw Pip still sitting among the roots.

“Thank you, Pip,” she called.

Pip did not say anything.

He just listened to the sound of Hazel’s voice, now full and calm, and smiled.

🌿
🌿 “Sometimes the smallest presence holds the most attention. And attention, when held gently, can guide us home.”
