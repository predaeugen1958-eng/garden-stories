The Badger and the Magpie

a story for curious minds (8–11 years)
🐾🖤

In the hollow of an old oak tree, at the edge of the big forest, two young ones were born in the same season.

One was a badger named Brokk — dark, low to the ground, with a white stripe running down his face like a river of moonlight. He was born in a burrow beneath the roots, warm and safe, surrounded by the smell of earth and moss.

The other was a magpie named Zia — sharp-eyed, black and white, with a voice that carried farther than her wings. She was born in the branches above, swaying with the wind, her first sight the open sky.

They did not meet as infants. But they grew up knowing of each other.

Brokk claimed the earth. He knew every root, every stone, every hidden passage beneath the forest floor. He could find the softest soil for digging, the sweetest roots for eating, the safest tunnels for sleeping.

Zia claimed the sky. She knew every breeze, every branch, every hollow where the wind would carry her farthest. She could spot a seed from above, a berry from afar, a hawk before it saw her.

They were rivals — not by choice, but by nature.

Brokk thought Zia was too loud, too careless, too quick to take what she had not earned.

Zia thought Brokk was too slow, too cautious, too rooted to see what lay beyond his own shadow.

They did not fight. They did not speak. They simply avoided each other, each believing they were the true owner of the forest.

❄️

Winter came early that year.

The forest grew quiet. The leaves fell. The streams froze. Brokk had stored enough food for the cold months — roots, nuts, a few mushrooms he had dried in the sun. He curled deep in his sett and waited.

Zia had not prepared. She had always relied on the abundance of autumn, on the generosity of the trees, on her sharp eyes to find what others missed. But this winter was harsher than any she remembered.

She grew thin. Her feathers lost their shine. She could not find enough to eat, and the cold crept into her bones.

One evening, she attempted to fly to a distant grove, hoping to find something — anything. A sharp pain shot through her wing. She had pulled a muscle, or perhaps worse. She could not fly far. She landed heavily on the ground, near Brokk's sett, and she could not rise.

🌿

Brokk heard her before he saw her.

A soft, ragged breathing, close to his entrance.

He peered out. There she lay — the proud magpie, reduced to a trembling heap of feathers, her wing dragging behind her.

He should have left her. She had never helped him. She had never been kind. She had taken what he could have used, claimed what he could have reached. She was not his family. She was not his friend.

But she was alive. And she was cold.

Brokk did not think. He acted.

He dragged her gently into his burrow — not by the wing, but by the loose feathers at her side. He placed her in the warmest corner of his sett, where the earth held the heat of the day. He brought her water, drop by drop, in a leaf he had shaped into a cup. He brought her food — the roots he had stored, the nuts he had gathered.

She did not speak. She could not.

He fed her for days.

❄️

Winter continued. Brokk grew thinner. Zia grew stronger.

At first, there was silence between them. Then, slowly, a few words.

"Why did you help me?" she asked, her voice rough from disuse.

Brokk did not answer immediately. He scratched the earth with his claws.

"I do not know," he said. "It was not a choice I made. It was something I did before I could decide."

Zia looked at him. "That is what it means to be good."

He did not know if that was true. But he knew that he did not regret it.

As the weeks passed, they began to speak more. She told him about the sky — how it looked from above, how the clouds moved, how the world seemed smaller and larger at the same time.

He told her about the earth — how the roots connected, how the soil held the memory of rain, how the forest was older than any tree.

She taught him to look up. He taught her to look down.

They were still different. But they were no longer enemies.

🐺

Spring came slowly, but it came.

The snow began to melt, and the forest stirred with new life. Brokk and Zia had grown close — not as rivals, but as companions. They shared the forest in a way they never had before.

But with spring came hunger.

A wolf had roamed into the forest from the northern hills. Lean, silent, and desperate. He had not eaten in days. And he had smelled the badger's sett.

One evening, as Brokk was digging for roots near the edge of the forest, he did not notice the shadow that had been following him for the past few minutes.

The wolf.

He was old, scarred, his ribs showing through his grey fur. He had been driven from his pack, and now he hunted alone.

Brokk lifted his head — and saw the wolf, low to the ground, eyes fixed on him.

He could not run. He was too far from his burrow, and the wolf was faster.

He braced himself.

But before the wolf could spring, a sharp cry cut through the air.

A black-and-white shape descended from the sky — fast, fierce, and loud. Zia swooped down, beating her wings in the wolf's face, screeching with a voice that seemed to come from every direction at once.

The wolf snarled, snapped at the air, but Zia was relentless. She dove again and again, pecking at his ears and eyes, her wings a blur of black and white.

The wolf retreated. He could not catch the badger with that wild creature screaming in his face.

Zia did not stop until the wolf was out of sight.

Then she landed beside Brokk, feathers ruffled, breathing hard.

"You fed me when I could not eat," she said, her voice still sharp from the fight. "I will not let you be eaten."

Brokk looked at her, still trembling from the near miss.

"That was dangerous," he said.

"So was feeding a stranger in winter," she replied.

They stood together in the fading light — Brokk, rooted and steady, and Zia, fierce and loyal.

And the forest, which had once seemed divided, now held them both.
